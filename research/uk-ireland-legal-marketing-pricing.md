## Executive summary: the decision grade numbers

1. **UK paid search for legal is the most expensive vertical in the country.** Real top of page bids: "personal injury solicitor" £21–£66, "no win no fee solicitor" £18–£57, "conveyancing solicitor" £9–£18, "family law solicitor" £6–£20. A PI or clinical negligence firm running Google Ads in the UK is looking at £40–£70 per click and often hundreds of pounds per qualified lead.

2. **Ireland is a structurally different, much smaller and cheaper paid market.** Search volumes run roughly 10–15x lower than the UK and clicks are far cheaper (PI tops out around €25, most practice areas €3–€10). This is partly population (5.1m vs 67m) and partly regulation (next point).

3. **Ireland bans the core UK personal injury ad playbook.** Irish solicitors legally cannot say "no win no fee," "no foal no fee," "free first consultation," cannot solicit or induce PI claims, and cannot advertise success rates (Solicitors Advertising Regulations 2019, enforced by the LSRA). The UK's number one paid legal term is illegal to run in Ireland. This pushes Irish legal marketing toward SEO, content, reputation and brand rather than aggressive PI paid search, and it is a genuine differentiator you can lead with.

4. **UK marketing budget benchmark for budget conversations: ~3% of turnover for marketing + business development**, not the ~1% accounting line some firms quote. A £10m turnover firm budgets around £300,000/year all in. Independent benchmarking (Armstrong Watson) runs higher at 4.4% of fee income, 5% for smaller firms. Anchor prospects to 3%+, the 1% figure lowballs the true budget roughly threefold.

5. **Website builds for solicitors:** England & Wales runs ~£6,500 (template/framework) to £14,500+ (bespoke) plus £200–£300+/month care plans. Ireland runs cheaper at roughly €3,500 for a standard 10 page build, €5,000–€7,500 all in once customised, with €50–€200/month care. Irish website pricing sits materially below the UK bespoke tier.

6. **SEO retainers:** UK legal £1,500–£5,000/month (sub £500 is a red flag). Ireland €1,200–€3,000/month for a small to medium firm, with a published entry tier from €450/month.

7. **PPC management:** priced either at 5–20% of ad spend (with a £250–£500 setup fee) or as flat retainers scaling from £200/month (freelancer, small budgets) to £5,000/month (mid budget agency on £5k–£20k/month spend).

8. **Two of your four target markets have thin data.** England & Wales and Republic of Ireland are well covered. Scotland and Northern Ireland have almost no public, jurisdiction specific pricing or budget data. That is a market gap, and arguably an opportunity, but do not assume UK averages transfer cleanly.

## 1. Demand side: what firms actually budget

### The benchmark to use

| Benchmark | Figure | Source | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Marketing + BD as % of turnover (the one to quote) | **~3%** (history 2.6–3.0%) | Law Firm Marketing Club, Professional Services Marketing Survey 2024 | Medium |
| Worked example, £10m turnover firm | **~£300,000/year** | Arithmetic on the 3% figure | Medium |
| Independent cross check | **4.4% of fee income** (5% for smaller firms) | Armstrong Watson | Medium |
| Narrow non payroll accounting line | **~1% of revenue** | Moore Kingston Smith, 211 mid market firms (£10m–£100m), FY2023 | **High (primary)** |

**The trap:** the 1% Moore Kingston Smith figure is real but it is a narrow accounting line that *excludes marketing and BD salaries* (those sit under staff costs at ~40%). If a prospect tells you "we only spend 1% on marketing," that is the bookkeeping line, not their true investment. Always reframe to the ~3% marketing+BD number or you will anchor the whole conversation too low.

### Estimated absolute budgets by firm size (UK proxy)

These come from legal marketing agency sources, not audited accounts, so treat as directional. They are the cleanest firm size segmentation available.

| Segment | Annual marketing budget (est.) | As % of revenue (est.) |
|---|---|---|
| Solo / high street (1–3 fee earners) | £3,000–£12,000 | ~3% |
| Small (4–15 fee earners) | £12,000–£40,000 | 2–3% |
| Mid size / regional (26–100 lawyers) | £40,000–£150,000 | 1.5–2.8% |
| Large commercial (100+ lawyers) | £150,000–£800,000+ | 1.5–3% |

**Channel split inside the budget** (one source, medium confidence): SEO ~45%, PPC ~30%, traditional ~15%, social ~10%. Most firms now put at least half the budget online.

### Per jurisdiction demand notes

- **England & Wales:** best data. Use 3% marketing+BD as the planning anchor.
- **Scotland:** the Law Society of Scotland runs a confidential financial benchmarking survey that explicitly covers marketing spend, for everyone from sole practitioners to large commercial firms. The data exists but is members only, so it is not quotable publicly. Apply E&W proxies, GBP.
- **Northern Ireland:** no public benchmark surfaced. Apply UK proxies, GBP, different regulator (Law Society of Northern Ireland).
- **Republic of Ireland:** no audited benchmark. Agency consensus suggests ~5%/year as the "serious" threshold (3% for steady, 8–10% for aggressive growth). Treat as opinion, not data. A real Irish benchmark (Law Society of Ireland or an accountancy equivalent) does not appear to be public and would be worth commissioning or estimating from your own client base.

### Management pricing (both markets, GBP anchored)

Two models:
- **Percentage of ad spend:** typically **5–15%** (some quote 10–20%), usually applied above ~£5,000/month spend, plus a **£250–£500 setup fee**. On small accounts this is often a minimum fee (£1,000–£2,000) rather than a true percentage.
- **Flat retainers that scale with spend:**

| Manager type | Management fee | Typical client ad spend |
|---|---|---|
| Freelancer | £200–£800/month | up to £2,000/month |
| Small budget agency | £500–£1,500/month | £1,000–£5,000/month |
| Mid budget agency | £1,500–£5,000/month | £5,000–£20,000/month |

Project based PPC/landing page work: £2,000–£15,000.

### The Ireland advertising restriction (critical for PI)

Under the **Solicitors Advertising Regulations 2019** (S.I. 229/2019), enforced by the **Legal Services Regulatory Authority (LSRA)** since 18 December 2020, Irish solicitors are **prohibited** from:
- Using "no win no fee," "no foal no fee," "free first consultation" or similar in any ad referring to personal injuries.
- Advertising that solicits, encourages or offers inducement to make personal injury claims.
- Quoting possible damages amounts not based on the PIAB Book of Quantum / statutory guidelines.
- Referring to a practitioner's "success rate."

**Implication:** the entire UK PI paid search strategy (the £57 "no win no fee" keyword, success rate ad copy, free consult hooks) is illegal in the Republic. Irish PI firms must compete on brand, content, reputation, local SEO and referral, not aggressive direct response. This is a constraint for the firms and a positioning advantage for an agency that understands it from day one. (Northern Ireland follows UK/SRA style rules, so the NI PI market behaves like the rest of the UK, another reason to keep NI and the Republic separate.)

## 9. Strategic implications for Solicitor Digital

1. **Lead with the budget reframe.** Walk into Irish and UK conversations anchoring on 3–5% of turnover, not the 1% accounting line. For a firm doing €1m, that is €30,000–€50,000/year, which comfortably supports a real retainer. Bring the Moore Kingston Smith and Armstrong Watson numbers as third party proof.

2. **Price the Republic against value, not the €450 floor.** The local market has trained Irish solicitors to expect cheap. Your US pedigree, the Juris Digital track record (250+ firms) and a productised, outcome focused offer justify a premium. A €2,000–€3,500/month all in retainer (web care + SEO + light paid) sits at the top of the current Irish band but is defensible.

3. **Own the advertising compliance angle in Ireland.** Most Irish firms and many local agencies treat the LSRA advertising rules as a vague constraint. Position Solicitor Digital as the agency that markets PI and litigation firms compliantly and effectively without the banned playbook. That is a wedge no US import competitor will have thought about.

4. **Sell SEO and brand harder than paid in Ireland.** The CPC and volume data show Irish paid search is shallow. Your margin and client results in the Republic will come from organic, local pack, content and reputation, which also dodges the advertising restrictions. Reserve aggressive paid search positioning for UK clients.

5. **Treat Northern Ireland as a UK market, not an Irish one.** GBP, SRA style advertising freedom (no win no fee is fine), UK CPCs. A Belfast PI firm can run the playbook a Dublin firm cannot. Keep separate pricing and messaging tracks.

6. **StoryBrand is an underexploited edge.** Few UK/Irish guides, US anchored pricing (£1,200–£7,900 equivalent), and you come from the market that invented the framework. Bundle BrandScript + wireframe into the website offer as a premium tier.

7. **Bundle the website + care plan + retainer.** Every robust data point shows the money is in recurring revenue (care plans £200–£600/€50–€500/month, SEO £1,500–£5,000/€1,200–€3,000/month), not one off builds. Price the build as the on ramp.

## 10. Gaps and what to research next

The data is genuinely thin in these areas. Do not quote confidently until filled:

1. **Scotland and Northern Ireland demand side.** No public budget data. The Law Society of Scotland benchmark is members only. Consider acquiring it via a member contact or estimating from your own pipeline.
2. **Audited Irish demand side spend.** Every Irish figure is a supply side rate card. A real Law Society of Ireland or accountancy benchmark would de risk pricing the priority market.
3. **Solo/high street and Top 200/City specifics.** Most clean data is the £10m–£100m mid market. The smallest and largest segments are largely estimated.
4. **Legal specific copywriting, video, photography and call tracking rates** in GBP/EUR. In scope, no figure survived.
5. **Single vendor risk in Ireland.** Website pricing leans heavily on MEANit/solicitorswebsites.ie and SEO on Digimark. Get 3+ fresh quotes to confirm the bands.
6. **PPC CPCs move fast.** Refresh the Google Ads pull quarterly, the legal auction is the most volatile in the market.
