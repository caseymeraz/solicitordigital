Multilingual websites

Multilingual Websites for Irish Solicitors: Beyond English Only

Ireland's population has changed faster than its solicitors' websites. Census after census shows hundreds of thousands of people in Ireland speaking a language other than English or Irish at home, with Polish consistently the largest group, alongside significant Romanian, Lithuanian, Portuguese and Spanish speaking communities. Almost every solicitor's website in the country remains English only.

That gap is an opportunity for the firms willing to close it first.

Why this matters for client acquisition

People dealing with a legal problem search in the language they think in. A Polish speaking family in Cork facing an employment dispute, or a Brazilian worker in Dublin after a workplace accident, will often search in their own language first, find little, and fall back to English results where every firm looks the same. A firm with a genuine Polish or Portuguese version of its site does not compete in that crowded English result. It stands nearly alone in the native language one.

The same logic applies to personal injury, family law, conveyancing for new communities buying first homes, and immigration work, where the client's preferred language is practically part of the case type.

What a credible multilingual site requires

The bar is higher than dropping a translate widget on the homepage. Widgets translate in the browser, which means search engines index nothing and the effort is invisible where it matters. A credible setup means real translated pages at their own URLs, translated page titles and descriptions, correct hreflang tags telling Google which page serves which language community, and translations that read naturally rather than mechanically, particularly for fee information, where the Law Society's transparency expectations apply in every language you publish.

It also means the intake side keeps the promise. A Polish language website answered only in English erodes the trust it built. Even one reliable arrangement, a bilingual staff member or a translation capable intake process, makes the difference.

The tooling has caught up

Historically the blocker was cost: professional translation of a full site, redone every time content changed, priced most firms out. That has changed. Our colleagues in the US built JurisLingo, a WordPress translation system designed for law firms, which produces legal aware translations across 14 languages including Polish, Portuguese, Romanian and Spanish, keeps every translation in the firm's own website rather than on a vendor's servers, and handles the technical SEO layer automatically. It was built for the American Spanish market first, and the same machinery applies directly to the languages Irish firms need.

First mover advantage is real here

In most Irish practice areas, being the first firm with a proper Polish or Portuguese site in your county is an achievable position, at modest cost, with the field almost entirely empty. Positions like that do not usually stay available for long.
