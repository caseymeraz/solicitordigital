The problem

Most firm websites are a brochure with a team page, and Google cannot rank a brochure

The typical Irish law firm site has a homepage, an about page, one services page that lists every practice area in a column, a team page with headshots, and a contact form. It looks respectable. It ranks for the firm's name and nothing else, because Google can only rank a page for what that page is about, and a page about nine things is about nothing.

Meanwhile the site is slow on a phone, the solicitor profiles are two lines and a photo, the second office is a line in the footer, and the form goes to an inbox nobody checks at five on a Friday.

A law firm website is an architecture first and a design second. One page per practice area, one profile per solicitor, one page per office, and the structure that ties them together.

Signs the current site is holding the firm back

  • Every practice area shares one page
  • Solicitor profiles that say almost nothing
  • An office that does not exist as far as Google is concerned
  • Slow to load on a phone, which is where clients are
  • A redesign in the past that lost rankings and nobody knows why
  • Nobody in the firm can update it without a developer

What we build

What a law firm website actually needs

The design matters. What matters more is the structure underneath it, because that is what decides whether each department ranks and whether visitors become enquiries.

Practice area architecture

A page for every practice area and sub pages for the work inside it, each written to rank for the searches clients actually make and to answer their first questions. This is the single biggest difference between a firm site that ranks and one that does not.

Solicitor profiles that carry weight

Qualifications, admissions, practice areas, notable work, memberships and links to the articles each solicitor has written. Google looks for evidence that real experts stand behind legal content, and referrals look up the person before they ring.

A page for every office

Each office gets its own page with address, hours, directions, the solicitors based there and the work it handles, tied to its own Google Business Profile, so every office can be found in its own local search.

Fast, mobile first, secure

Most clients arrive on a phone. We build to pass Core Web Vitals, load in well under three seconds on mobile, run on HTTPS and stay fast as the site grows to hundreds of pages.

Built to convert, and measured

Clear calls to action on every page, short forms, click to call, and tracking on calls and forms tied into GA4 so the firm can see which department each enquiry came from and what it cost.

Compliant and owned by you

LSRA compliant copy, GDPR compliant forms and cookie consent, accessibility built in, and a content management system your staff can use. The domain, hosting and site belong to the firm, and we hand over the keys at launch.

Redesigning an existing site

A redesign should raise your rankings, not reset them

The most expensive mistake in law firm web design is a beautiful new site that launches and quietly loses the rankings the old one had, because the pages that ranked were deleted or moved without redirects. We have taken over enough of those to know exactly how it happens.

Every rebuild we do starts with a map of what already ranks. Those pages are kept or improved, every old address is redirected to its new home, and Search Console is checked before and after launch. Done properly, a redesign usually improves rankings because the structure gets better.

What our migration process covers

  • Inventory of every existing page and what it ranks for
  • Redirect map from every old address to its new one
  • Practice area and profile content kept or rewritten, never dropped
  • Analytics, call tracking and Search Console carried across
  • Launch checks on speed, indexing and forms
  • A follow up review of rankings in the weeks after launch

Built for Irish firms

A website that stays inside the LSRA rules and GDPR

A law firm website is advertising under the Legal Services Regulatory Authority rules and the Solicitors Advertising Regulations. Everything on it has to be truthful, professional and not misleading, with particular restrictions around personal injury work. On a firm site with dozens of pages and several authors, that needs a review step, not good intentions.

We build the check into the process, and we handle GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018 properly: compliant forms and privacy notices, cookie consent that meets Irish standards, and secure handling of the enquiries the site collects.

Compliance areas we cover

  • LSRA advertising restrictions, including personal injury
  • Truthful, non misleading claims and outcome language
  • Proper disclaimers and regulatory information
  • GDPR compliant forms, privacy notice and data handling
  • Cookie consent that meets Irish standards
  • Accessibility for clients who need it
You focus on practising law. We handle the website and keep it compliant, end to end.

How long does a law firm website take to build?▼
Typically 6 to 12 weeks for a firm site, depending on how much content is new, how many people need to approve it and how quickly the firm can supply profile details and photographs. The content and approvals, not the build, are usually what set the timeline.
Will we lose our Google rankings if we redesign the website?▼
Not if the migration is done properly, and this is where most law firm redesigns go wrong. We map every existing page to its new address, set the redirects, keep the pages that already rank, and check Search Console before and after launch. A redesign done well usually improves rankings because the structure gets better.
